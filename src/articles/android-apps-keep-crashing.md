Few things are more frustrating than tapping an app and watching it close straight away, freeze on a white screen, or pop up a message saying it "keeps stopping". The good news is that most app crashes on Android are fixed by a handful of simple steps, and you rarely need to lose any data.

This guide is ordered **safest first**. Work through the steps in order and test the app after each one. Menu names differ between manufacturers (Samsung, Pixel, Motorola, Xiaomi and others), so if a label doesn't match, type the setting's name into the search bar at the top of **Settings**.

**One app or lots of apps?** If only one app misbehaves, the problem is usually with that app. If several apps crash at once, start with Steps 1, 2 and 6, because the cause is often shared – such as low storage or outdated system components.

## Step 1: Restart your phone

A restart clears out temporary glitches and frees up memory. Hold the **Power** button and tap **Restart**. If the phone is frozen, Google's advice is to force a restart: on many phones you hold the Power button for about 30 seconds until it restarts. On Pixel 6 and later models, press and hold **Power** and **Volume up** for a few seconds, then tap **Restart**.

Open the app again once the phone is back on. A surprising number of crashes stop here.

## Step 2: Check your free storage

Android and its apps need working space. Google notes that problems can start when less than 10% of your storage is free. Open **Settings › Storage** to see how much space is left.

If you're short of space, our guide to [freeing up Android storage safely](/articles/android-storage-full/) walks you through backing up photos, clearing downloads and removing large apps without deleting the wrong things.

## Step 3: Update the app and Android

Developers regularly fix crash bugs, and an app can also break if it's much newer or older than your version of Android.

**Update the app:**

1. Open the **Play Store**.
2. Tap your profile icon at the top right.
3. Tap **Manage apps & devices**.
4. Under **Updates available**, update the app (or update all apps).

**Update Android:** open **Settings › System › Software update** (on some phones it's under **About phone › Software update**, or **System › Advanced › System update**) and install anything offered. Connect to Wi-Fi and plug in the charger first, as system updates can be large.

## Step 4: Force stop the app

If an app has got stuck in a bad state, forcing it to close completely gives it a clean start. On a Pixel the path is:

1. Open **Settings › Apps › See all apps**.
2. Tap the app that's crashing.
3. Tap **Force stop**, then **OK**.

Other phones use a very similar route through **Settings › Apps**. Force stopping doesn't delete anything – the app simply reopens fresh next time.

## Step 5: Clear the app's cache

The cache holds temporary files that help an app load quickly. If those files become corrupted, the app can crash on launch. Clearing the cache is safe: you stay signed in and your settings remain.

1. Go to **Settings › Apps › See all apps** and tap the app.
2. Tap **Storage & cache** (just **Storage** on some phones).
3. Tap **Clear cache**.

Google describes this as a short-term fix, so if crashes return regularly, carry on with the steps below.

## Step 6: Update Google Play services

Many apps rely on **Google Play services** for sign-in, location, notifications and payments. If it's out of date, lots of different apps can fail at once. You can't force stop or uninstall it, but you can update it:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap your profile picture, then **All services**.
3. Under **Privacy & security**, tap **System services › Google Play services**.
4. Tap **Update** or **Install** if either appears.

If you don't see these options on your phone, don't worry – Play services normally updates itself in the background. Restart the phone and test again.

## Step 7: Clear the app's storage (resets the app)

> **Warning:** Clear storage (called **Clear data** on some phones) resets the app as if it were newly installed. Google warns that any data saved in the app will be erased. You'll need to sign in again, and offline downloads, drafts or game progress that isn't backed up online may be lost. Check whether the app has its own backup option first – messaging apps, for example, usually do.

If you're happy to proceed:

1. Go to **Settings › Apps › See all apps** and tap the app.
2. Tap **Storage & cache**.
3. Tap **Clear storage** (or **Clear data**), then **OK**.

## Step 8: Use safe mode to find a problem app

Sometimes the app that crashes isn't the culprit – another app, such as a "cleaner", overlay or launcher, interferes with it. **Safe mode** temporarily turns off all downloaded apps so you can test this.

> **Before you start:** safe mode removes some Home screen widgets. Take a screenshot of your Home screen so you can put them back afterwards.

How you enter safe mode varies by phone, so check your manufacturer's support site. On a **Pixel 6 or later**, press and hold **Power** and **Volume up**, then touch and hold **Power off** or **Restart** until you're asked about safe mode, and tap **OK**. "Safe mode" then appears at the bottom of the screen. On Pixel phones, aeroplane mode is turned on automatically in safe mode, so turn it off if you need Wi-Fi or calls.

Safe mode only runs pre-installed apps, so you can test a built-in app (such as the camera or messages) directly. If the phone as a whole is much more stable in safe mode, a downloaded app is likely causing trouble. Restart normally, then remove recently installed apps **one at a time**, restarting after each, until the problem stops. You can reinstall the ones you need afterwards.

## Step 9: Uninstall and reinstall the app

A fresh installation replaces any damaged app files.

> **Warning:** uninstalling erases any data saved only inside the app. Make sure anything important is synced or backed up first.

Touch and hold the app icon, tap **Remove App** or **Uninstall** (wording varies), and confirm. Restart the phone, then install the app again from the Play Store. Only install apps from trusted sources – the same principle we cover in our guide to [installing software safely on Windows](/articles/install-software-safely-windows/) applies to phones too.

Some pre-installed apps can't be removed. On a Pixel you can instead touch and hold the app, tap **App info**, open the three-dot menu and choose **Uninstall updates**, then update it again from the Play Store. Note that this also erases the app's data.

## Step 10: Contact the developer

If an app still crashes after a clean reinstall on an up-to-date phone, the bug is probably in the app itself. Open the app's page in the Play Store and use the developer contact details to report it, including your phone model and Android version. A fix often arrives in the next update.

## When the whole phone is unstable

If your phone restarts on its own or crashes across the board, even in safe mode, the problem is bigger than one app. Google's advice includes removing cases and external batteries and, as a last resort, a factory data reset – which **removes all data from your phone**, so back up everything first. Battery problems can also cause instability; see our [Android battery guide](/articles/android-battery-draining-fast/) if your phone is also losing charge quickly.

## Conclusion

Most "app keeps stopping" errors are fixed by restarting, freeing up storage, updating the app and Android, then force stopping and clearing the cache. Only move on to clearing storage or reinstalling once you know your data is safe, and use safe mode if you suspect another app is causing trouble. Working through the steps in order solves the vast majority of crashes without a factory reset.
