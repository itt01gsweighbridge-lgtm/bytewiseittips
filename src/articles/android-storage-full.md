When your Android phone runs out of storage, apps slow down, photos won’t save and updates fail to install. The fix is usually not deleting at random but finding the few things taking up most of the space. Settings vary between manufacturers, so use the search bar in **Settings** if a menu name doesn’t match.

> **Before deleting anything important, make sure it’s backed up.** Photos, videos and documents are the hardest things to replace. See our [3-2-1 backup guide](/articles/3-2-1-backup-rule/).

## Step 1: See what’s using your storage

Open **Settings › Storage** (on Samsung: **Settings › Battery and device care › Storage**, or **Settings › Device care › Storage** on older versions). You’ll see a breakdown by category such as **Images**, **Videos**, **Apps**, **Documents**, **Audio** and **System**.

Tap each category to see the biggest items. Photos and videos, messaging app media and large games are usually the main culprits.

## Step 2: Back up photos and videos, then free up space

Photos and videos are typically the largest category.

**Using Google Photos:**

1. Open Google Photos and make sure **backup** is turned on (tap your profile picture to check the status).
2. Wait until it says backup is complete.
3. Tap your profile picture and choose **Free up space** (sometimes shown as *Free up space on this device*). This removes only the device copies of items that are safely backed up.

Other cloud services such as Microsoft OneDrive or your manufacturer’s cloud can do the same job. Alternatively, connect your phone to a computer with a USB cable and copy the **DCIM** folder to the computer and an external drive.

Videos are especially large – a few minutes of 4K video can take up several gigabytes. Review your longest videos first.

## Step 3: Use Files by Google (or your phone’s file manager)

The **Files by Google** app (pre-installed on many phones and free on the Play Store) has a **Clean** tab that suggests:

- junk and temporary files;
- duplicate files;
- large files;
- old screenshots and downloaded files;
- media from messaging apps.

Review each suggestion before confirming – it lists exactly what will be deleted. Samsung’s **My Files** app has a similar storage analysis feature.

## Step 4: Clear out Downloads

The **Download** folder collects PDFs, installers, images and attachments you opened once and forgot. Open your file manager, go to **Downloads**, sort by size, and delete what you don’t need.

## Step 5: Manage messaging app media

WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal and similar apps save every photo, video and voice note you receive – including group chat memes.

- **WhatsApp:** go to **Settings › Storage and data › Manage storage** to see which chats use the most space and review large files. Turn off **Media auto-download** on mobile data and Wi-Fi if you don’t want every file saved automatically.
- Other apps have similar options in their storage or data settings.

## Step 6: Clear app caches

Apps store temporary files (cache) that can grow large, especially browsers, social media and streaming apps.

1. Go to **Settings › Apps** and select an app.
2. Tap **Storage & cache** (or **Storage**).
3. Tap **Clear cache**.

Avoid **Clear storage** / **Clear data** unless you want to reset the app – it signs you out and can delete offline content and settings.

## Step 7: Remove apps you don’t use

Go to **Settings › Apps**, sort by size if available, and uninstall apps you no longer use. The Play Store also helps: open it, tap your profile picture, choose **Manage apps & device › Manage**, and sort by size or by least used. Some phones offer **Archive apps** (or automatic archiving), which removes most of an app while keeping your data so you can restore it later.

## Step 8: Check offline downloads

Streaming and music apps (video services, Spotify, podcasts, maps) can store large offline downloads. Open each app’s downloads section and remove content you’ve already watched or listened to. Offline map areas in navigation apps can also be large.

## Step 9: Use a microSD card (if supported)

Some Android phones have a microSD card slot. You can set the camera to save new photos to the card and move media files there. Avoid cheap, unbranded cards, which can fail and lose data.

## Step 10: Restart and update

A restart clears some temporary files. Keeping Android updated is also important – but updates need free space to install, so freeing space first often fixes failed updates.

## What not to do

- Don’t install “cleaner” or “booster” apps from unknown developers – many show aggressive ads and some collect data. The built-in tools and Files by Google do the job.
- Don’t delete folders you don’t recognise in the file manager, especially **Android** or system folders.
- Don’t factory reset just to free space unless you have a full backup – and it’s rarely necessary.

## Conclusion

Most Android storage problems come down to photos, videos, messaging app media and a few large apps. Back up your photos and use **Free up space**, clean downloads and messaging media, clear caches, and remove apps you don’t use. A few minutes of tidying usually recovers several gigabytes.
