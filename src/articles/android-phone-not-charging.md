A phone that won’t charge feels like an emergency, but the cause is often minor: a damaged cable, a weak charger or lint packed into the charging port. Work through these steps in order – most people find the problem within the first three.

> **Safety warning:** if your phone is swollen, very hot, smells unusual, or the screen is lifting away from the frame, stop charging it immediately and take it to an authorised repair centre. Don’t try to charge a phone that has recently been in water.

## Step 1: Try a different cable

Cables fail far more often than phones. The wires inside can break near the connectors even when the outside looks fine. Try another cable you know works – ideally the one that came with the phone or a good-quality USB-C cable from a reputable brand.

## Step 2: Try a different charger and socket

- Plug the charger into a different wall socket.
- Try a different charger, preferably one designed for your phone.
- Avoid charging from a computer’s USB port or a car adapter while testing – they often supply much less power.

If the phone charges with a different charger, the original adapter is faulty.

## Step 3: Inspect and clean the charging port

Pockets and bags fill USB-C ports with lint, which stops the cable seating fully. Signs include a cable that feels loose, falls out easily, or only charges at a certain angle.

1. **Turn the phone off.**
2. Shine a torch into the port to look for debris or corrosion.
3. Gently blow into the port, or use short bursts of compressed air from a distance.
4. If lint is packed in, very carefully loosen it with a **wooden or plastic toothpick**, scraping lightly along the sides – never the centre contact – and never using metal objects such as pins or paperclips.

If you see green or white corrosion, or the port appears damaged, have it repaired professionally.

## Step 4: Restart the phone

Software glitches can stop a phone from recognising a charger. Press and hold the power button (or power and volume up on some phones) and choose **Restart**. If the screen is unresponsive, most Android phones force-restart if you hold the **power and volume down** buttons for 10–20 seconds.

## Step 5: Check for “Moisture detected” warnings

Many phones block wired charging when they sense moisture in the port. If you see a moisture alert:

- unplug the cable;
- dry the outside of the phone and let it sit with the port facing down in a dry, ventilated place for a few hours;
- don’t use a hairdryer, oven or rice – heat can damage the phone and rice can leave dust in the port.

If your phone supports **wireless charging**, it usually still works while the port dries. If the warning persists when the phone is definitely dry, the port may need cleaning or repair.

## Step 6: Charge with the phone off or in Safe Mode

If the phone charges very slowly, an app or heavy use may be draining power as fast as it charges. Turn the phone off and charge it for 30 minutes to test.

To check whether a downloaded app is causing problems, start the phone in **Safe Mode** – press and hold the power button, then press and hold **Power off** until the Safe Mode option appears. If charging behaves normally in Safe Mode, remove recently installed apps one at a time.

## Step 7: Check temperature and charging settings

Phones deliberately slow or pause charging when they’re **too hot or too cold** to protect the battery. Remove thick cases while charging, keep the phone out of direct sunlight and avoid gaming while charging.

Some features intentionally limit charging:

- **Adaptive or optimised charging** may hold the battery at around 80% overnight and finish just before your usual alarm.
- **Battery protection** or **charge limit** settings can cap charging at 80–85% permanently.

Check **Settings › Battery** (or **Battery and device care** on Samsung) to see if these are on – they’re good for battery health, but can look like a fault if you don’t know they’re active.

## Step 8: Update your software

If the phone charges but the battery runs down unusually quickly, our guide to [Android battery drain](/articles/android-battery-draining-fast/) explains how to find the cause. Install any available updates under **Settings › System › Software update** (or **Settings › Software update**). Charging and battery management bugs are sometimes fixed in updates.

## Step 9: Try wireless charging (if supported)

If wireless charging works but cable charging doesn’t, the problem is almost certainly the **charging port** or cable rather than the battery.

## Step 10: Get it repaired

Seek professional repair if:

- the phone won’t charge with any cable, charger or method;
- the port is visibly damaged, corroded or loose;
- the battery percentage jumps around or the phone shuts down at 20–30%;
- the phone has been dropped or exposed to water.

Charging port and battery replacements are common, relatively inexpensive repairs. Use an authorised or reputable repair service, and back up your phone first if it still turns on.

## Conclusion

Start with the cheapest explanations: a different cable, a different charger and a clean port. Then rule out software, moisture and temperature limits. If none of these help, the port or battery likely needs replacing – a routine repair that is usually far cheaper than a new phone.
