If your Android phone used to last all day and now needs charging by mid-afternoon, something has changed – an app misbehaving, a setting, a recent update, or an ageing battery. Android includes the tools to find out which. Menu names vary between manufacturers (Samsung, Google Pixel, Motorola, Xiaomi and others), so if a setting isn’t exactly where described, use the search bar at the top of **Settings**.

## Step 1: Look at battery usage

Open **Settings › Battery** and look for **Battery usage** (on some phones, tap the battery graph or **View detailed usage**). This shows which apps and system components used the most power since the last full charge or over the past 24 hours.

Look for:

- **An app you barely used** near the top of the list – it’s working in the background.
- **Screen** as the biggest consumer – normal if you use your phone a lot; display settings will help.
- **Mobile network or “Phone idle”** high on the list – often a weak signal problem (see Step 5).

## Step 2: Deal with power-hungry apps

For an app using far more battery than it should:

1. Tap it in the battery usage list, or go to **Settings › Apps** and select it.
2. Open **Battery** (or **App battery usage**).
3. Choose **Restricted** or **Optimised** to limit background activity. (Samsung phones also let you put apps into **Sleeping** or **Deep sleeping** lists.)

Also:

- **Update the app** from the Play Store – battery bugs are frequently fixed in updates.
- **Force stop** it once if it seems stuck, then reopen it.
- **Uninstall** apps you no longer use.

Be careful restricting messaging, email, alarm or health apps – they may stop delivering notifications on time.

## Step 3: Turn on Adaptive Battery and Battery Saver

- **Adaptive Battery** (on many phones under **Settings › Battery**) learns which apps you use and limits the ones you don’t. Leave it on.
- **Battery Saver** or **Power saving mode** reduces background activity and visual effects when you need the charge to last longer. Many phones can turn it on automatically at a set percentage.

## Step 4: Adjust display settings

The screen is usually the largest power user.

- Turn on **Adaptive brightness** and avoid running at maximum brightness indoors.
- Reduce the **Screen timeout** to 30 seconds or one minute.
- Use **Dark theme** – on phones with OLED screens (most mid-range and flagship models), dark pixels use less power.
- If your phone has a high refresh rate display (90 Hz or 120 Hz), choosing **Standard** or **60 Hz**, or an adaptive mode, saves power.
- Turn off **Always-on display** if you don’t need it.

## Step 5: Check connectivity and location

- **Weak mobile signal** forces the phone’s radio to work harder. In areas with poor reception, using Wi-Fi (and Wi-Fi calling, if your carrier supports it) helps.
- **5G** can use more power than 4G in some areas. Some phones offer a setting to prefer 4G/LTE.
- **Location**: review **Settings › Location › App location permissions** and change apps that don’t need constant location to **Allow only while using the app**.
- Turn off **Bluetooth**, **NFC** and **hotspot** when not needed – though modern Bluetooth uses relatively little power.

## Step 6: Install system updates

Go to **Settings › System › Software update** (or **Settings › Software update** on Samsung) and install anything available. Updates often include battery optimisations. Note that battery life can be worse for a day or two **after** a major update while the phone re-optimises apps in the background – give it time before judging.

## Step 7: Restart and check for problem apps in Safe Mode

A restart clears runaway processes. If drain continues, boot into **Safe Mode**, which temporarily disables downloaded apps. On most phones, press and hold the power button (or power and volume up), then **press and hold “Power off”** until the Safe Mode prompt appears. If battery drain stops in Safe Mode, a downloaded app is responsible – restart normally and remove recently installed apps one at a time.

## Step 8: Check battery health

Lithium-ion batteries wear out with charge cycles and age. After two or three years, noticeably shorter battery life is normal.

- Some phones show **Battery health** or capacity in **Settings › Battery**.
- Samsung users can run battery diagnostics in the **Samsung Members** app.
- Turn on **adaptive/optimised charging** or a **charge limit** (for example 80–85%) if available, to slow future wear.

> If the phone gets very hot, the case bulges or the screen lifts, stop using and charging it and take it to an authorised repair centre. A swollen battery is a safety hazard.

A battery replacement at an authorised service centre is often much cheaper than a new phone.

## Conclusion

Start with the battery usage screen – it usually points straight at the cause. Restrict misbehaving apps, tune display and location settings, keep software updated and use your phone’s battery-protection features. If the battery is simply worn out, a professional replacement can restore all-day battery life.
